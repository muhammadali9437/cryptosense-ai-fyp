## How prices go up and down
A crypto price is simply the last price someone paid. On an exchange, buyers post the price they are willing to pay and sellers post the price they are willing to accept. When the two meet, a trade happens and that becomes the new price. If more people want to buy than sell, buyers have to offer more and the price climbs. If more want to sell, sellers accept less and it falls. News, fear, excitement, big holders moving money, and patterns many traders watch change how eager each side is. Takeaway: price moves when one side is more eager than the other.

## Reading candlesticks
A candle shows four prices for one slice of time: open, high, low and close. The thick body connects open and close. Green means the close was higher; red means it was lower. A long wick below the body means sellers pushed the price down but buyers pushed it back up. A long wick above means buyers tried to push higher and sellers pushed back. Several tall green candles show strong buying; small candles with long wicks on both sides show indecision. Takeaway: bodies show who won; wicks show where the fight happened.

## Trends, support and resistance
An uptrend is a series of higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend is lower highs and lower lows. Support is a level where buyers stepped in before, like a floor. Resistance is a level where sellers appeared before, like a ceiling. When price breaks resistance with strong volume, the old ceiling often becomes the new floor. A break without volume is more likely to fail. Takeaway: trade with the trend and watch levels where price turned before.

## Momentum and RSI
Momentum is the speed of a move. RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures momentum from 0 to 100. Above 70 the move is stretched upward; below 30 it is stretched downward. These zones mean be careful chasing, not buy or sell now. Divergence (price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high) shows buying pressure weakening. Takeaway: RSI tells you how tired a move is, not where it will end.

## Volatility and managing risk
Crypto can move 10% in a day. Volatility decides how carefully you size positions. A common rule is to risk only 1 to 2 percent of your total money on any single idea. With $1,000 and a 2% rule you accept losing $20; with a 10% stop-loss your position should be about $200. Always choose the stop-loss before buying, then work out the position size. Takeaway: decide how much you can lose first.

## Whales, news and hype
Whales hold so much that their trades alone move prices. Coins sent to exchanges may be sold; coins pulled off exchanges usually mean holding. News moves prices fast and the first reaction is often too strong. Hype is the most dangerous force, especially with new coins: when everyone talks about a coin that already doubled, most of the easy gain is gone. Takeaway: use whales and news as clues and be most careful when excitement is highest.

## Funding rate and futures traders
On futures exchanges, the funding rate is a small fee paid every 8 hours between traders betting on a rise (longs) and a fall (shorts). Positive funding means most traders are betting on a rise; very high funding often comes before a sharp drop that shakes them out. Negative funding means traders lean short, and a sudden rise can force them to buy back (a short squeeze). Takeaway: when everyone leans one way, the market often surprises them.

## Fear and Greed Index
The Crypto Fear and Greed Index (0 to 100) combines volatility, volume, social media, surveys and search trends. Extreme fear (below 25) often appears near bottoms when people panic; extreme greed (above 75) often appears near tops when people are overexcited. Takeaway: be careful when others are greedy and pay attention when others are fearful.
